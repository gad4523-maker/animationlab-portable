# Action and editing

## Staging an action

- Set the gaze before the body follows. Put weight through a planted foot and shift the hips before acceleration. Send the action from pelvis to chest to arm and let hair, sleeves, and straps trail on their own timing.
- Assign four frame markers for anticipation, release, contact, and recovery. Set those poses first. Use spaced breakdowns at the fastest portion. On twos, step only the character within the shot when it supports the aesthetic. Keep the camera and the effects on the delivery clock.
- Aim the contact silhouette at the target. Confirm both entities appear and read together before the hit. Use a brief directional smear that connects the source and destination. Use a short readable impact pose, optionally followed by a small hitstop while independent effects continue. Treat frame counts as starting points. A 1–3-frame smear and 2–4-frame hold at 30 fps can work if the action remains clear.
- Keep recovery in frame. Carry momentum into the hips, ground contact, cloth, and sound. Check hands and feet separately at the fastest frames, along with neck and knee angles. Avoid a second full-frame brightness peak that competes with the contact.

## Edit, sound, and loop

Choose cut points around action or changes in character intention. Use a sound lead for anticipation when it helps comprehension. Place the snap or transient on the visible contact, with secondary echoes and debris after it. Number cues on the same frame grid used for shots and transition overlaps. Keep any score or ambience under the cue that carries story information.

For a loop, decide what the ending changes about the opening. Match screen-space pose, camera, light, and optical velocity at the wrap or cover the junction with a motivated occluder. Use integer phase periods that divide the number of output frames. For absolute time, use the frame index and wrap it rather than advancing accumulated simulation state. Export frames 0 through N−1. Compare N−2, N−1, 0, 1 with the audio join and test the replay at speed.
