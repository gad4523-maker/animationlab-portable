# Direct anime Shorts

1. Default to **HD-2D pixel cinema** and read [the HD-2D guide](references/hd2d-pixel-cinema.md) before visual planning. Only change the art style when the user explicitly asks. Infer genre and emotional rhythm from this anime/topic; never treat HD-2D as a thriller preset. Define the opening question, what the character wants, what changes, and the strongest image the film earns. Build the shot list backward from that image. Give each shot a new observation, decision, or consequence.
2. Lock the episode's HD-2D pixel scale, silhouette, palette roles, key light direction, shadow bands, atmosphere, and texture before rendering (or follow the user's explicit alternate art style). Select the renderer per shot for the asset and movement. **Do not generate images or videos with generative AI, including sprites, sprite sheets, textures, backgrounds, character art and individual frames.** Use original procedural/code art, manually authored artwork or appropriately licensed human-made assets. Animate them in the engines; never substitute a static-image slideshow for animation.
3. Work on a frame clock. Block anticipation, contact and recovery as readable silhouettes. Plant supporting feet while weight shifts. Establish facial intent before big action. Add arcs, smear drawings at peak speed, and a brief contact hold with secondary motion. Key scarf, hair, and debris slightly behind the body.
4. Compose at phone size in 9:16. Keep the subject's face and the action target clear of captions in the bottom 22.5% and UI in the right 12.5%. Stage parallax and camera movement around the focal point. Match action direction, eye lines, pose, palette, and key-light direction across cuts.
5. Cut to changes in information or tension. Cue sound at contact and let quiet create contrast. Tie the last image or question back to the opening if the story supports a loop.
6. Review one focused draft with sound. Check exact impact neighbors, hand and foot contact, intersections, anatomy, facial clarity, effect hierarchy, caption comfort, and shot-to-shot shading. Measure rendering cost on the intended profile. Deliver the selected settings and verify frame count, full decode, audio sync, and peak headroom.
